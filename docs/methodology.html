Today Signals Performance Methodology

What this is

A hobby model that projects strikeout totals for every MLB starting pitcher, compares them to sportsbook over/under lines, and ranks any positive-expected-value edges. It runs itself on a nightly cycle: every evening after most games finish it scores the completed slate and projects the following day, and every morning it pulls the day's betting lines. Every projection is logged before games start and results are reconciled automatically. Nothing is ever deleted or cherry-picked — the Performance page is the whole record.

The update cycle

  • 9:10 PM AZ — nightly model refresh. Runs after most games are final: ingests the day's box scores, scores every logged projection, settles picks, and projects the following day's probable starters. This is when the board flips to tomorrow.
  • 10:10 AM AZ — morning odds run. Pulls game totals/moneylines and K prop lines for the day's slate from US books (free-tier credit budget), settles any West-coast games that finished after the nightly run, and rescores edges against the fresh lines.
  • Hourly, ~7 AM–9 PM AZ — Signals feed. A lighter job refreshes the Signals tab: capper posts, lineup status, and pick windows.
  • Sunday overnight — weekly retrain on every start since 2022.
  • Manual: lines typed into lines/manual_lines.csv rescore within a couple of minutes; a "Run workflow" tap in the GitHub app forces a full refresh any time (an afternoon manual run also re-pulls props for the top-edge games to capture line movement).

One pick, one book

The same bet is usually offered at half a dozen books at slightly different prices — for bookkeeping that is one pick, not six. Everything on the Performance and Signals pages is scored against a single canonical book per pick: DraftKings when it has a line (the most consistent coverage in our odds feed), otherwise the next book down a fixed chain (FanDuel, BetMGM, Caesars, BetRivers, Bovada, BetOnline). All books are still ingested and stored — the Today page simply shows the best available price for each pick, tagged with its book.

The model

A LightGBM gradient-boosted tree model with a Poisson objective produces the point estimate, and five separate quantile models (p10/p25/p50/p75/p90) trace the predictive distribution. A smooth monotone curve (PCHIP) through those quantiles gives the probability of clearing any line. It retrains weekly on an expanding window of every start since 2022.

Features, in order of importance

  • Pitcher swing-and-miss form: CSW% (called strikes + whiffs per pitch), SwStr%, and K% as exponentially-weighted averages with a half-life of ~5 starts. CSW% stabilizes much faster than K%, which is why it leads.
  • Opponent lineup K% vs handedness: slot-weighted strikeout rate of the nine projected hitters against the pitcher's hand, blending the last 30 days (60%) with the season (40%).
  • Expected workload: recent batters-faced average plus the game's run environment (Vegas total when available, otherwise both teams' recent scoring).
  • Stabilizers and context: K-BB%, fastball velocity trend (fatigue flag), park K factor, home-plate umpire tendency, temperature/wind for outdoor parks, home/away, days of rest.
  • Early-season prior: a Marcel-style regressed prior from last season, blended by sample size — the model leans on the prior in April and on current form by June (weight = BF / (BF + 70)).
  • Deliberately excluded: batter-vs-pitcher history (mostly noise under ~25 PA).

When lineups aren't posted yet

Lineup cards usually post 30–90 minutes before first pitch. Until then the model falls back: confirmed lineup → most-used hitters over the last 7 days → team-average K% vs hand. Each tier carries a lineup confidence (1.0 / 0.6 / 0.3) shown on every card, and lower confidence widens the projected range and discounts the edge score rather than pretending certainty.

Edge math & pick ranking

For each K line we strip the vig from the two-way price (multiplicative method) to get the market's true win probability, and read the model's win probability off the projected distribution. The difference is the probability edge:

prob edge = model win % − vig-free market win %

Picks are ranked by probability edge, biggest first — it directly answers "how much more often does the model think this bet wins than the market does?" Each pick also shows the model's win %, expected value per unit, and a quarter-Kelly stake for sizing. Only positive-EV sides are surfaced — a high win probability at a price that still loses money long-run (heavy vig) is not a pick.

How to read the Signals tab

Signals is the pre-game validation view: is this pick ready to bet, and does anyone else agree?

  • Pick window: every starter gets a GO / CAUTION / WAIT / OFF state. GO requires a probability edge of at least 5 points, both lineups posted, and a fresh K line. CAUTION and WAIT list exactly what's missing — stale line, lineups not posted, or the line moved against the model. OFF means no line yet or a scratched starter.
  • Model / Line / FanGraphs row: three independent per-start K numbers — our projection, the canonical book's line, and FanGraphs Depth Charts converted to a per-start figure. Three arrows pointing the same way is corroboration; the model standing alone is a reason for extra care.
  • Capper picks: K-prop picks pulled best-effort from five X accounts (public mirrors go down; lines/capper_picks.csv is the manual fallback). Each pick is tagged "model agrees / disagrees" with our win %.
  • Capper scorecard: every parsed capper pick is settled against the official MLB box score at 1u flat — the same rules our own record uses, so the comparison is honest.
  • Source health chips: when each feed (props, cappers, FanGraphs, lineups) last succeeded — a quiet card means "no data", not "no edge".

How to read the Performance tab

Performance is the after-the-fact record. Everything on it follows the one-pick-one-book rule.

  • Betting record: all settled positive-EV picks at 1u flat — ROI, hit rate, net units, and closing-line value where a closing line was recorded. The low-sample warning stays up until ~500 settled picks.
  • Model vs the market: drops the +EV filter and asks a harder question: on every canonical line, take whichever side the model favored — what's the record and net units, and who called the Ks closer, the model or the book (MAE, with a monthly breakdown)?
  • Projection accuracy: MAE and bias of the raw K projections plus p10–p90 coverage (target ≈ 80%) — model quality independent of any betting.
  • Calibration: predicted win % vs what actually happened, in buckets. Dots on the diagonal mean the probabilities can be taken at face value.
  • Recent picks & model versions: the raw pick log — one line per pick at its canonical book — and each trained model's backtest vs live error.

Honest limitations

  • K prop lines pull once each morning (free-tier credit budget). The targeted line-movement re-pull only fires on manual afternoon runs now, so lines can go stale by evening — the Signals tab shows line freshness for exactly this reason.
  • Most games are final by the 9:10 PM AZ refresh, but late West-coast games settle on the next morning's run.
  • CLV is only measured when a closing line is entered manually.
  • Capper tracking is best-effort scraping of public mirrors — gaps happen and are shown as source-health warnings, not silently filled.
  • Retractable-roof parks: day-of roof status isn't freely available, so weather is attenuated rather than exact.
  • Trust the calibration plot and 30-day panel over any single hot streak; below ~500 settled picks the ROI number is mostly luck either way.
